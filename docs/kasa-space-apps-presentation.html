KasaBe an Earth System Trend Detective
01 / The problem

Environmental change is difficult to read.

Satellite imagery is powerful, but a picture alone does not tell us whether a change is meaningful, temporary, or just noise. Climate data is valuable, but often difficult to interpret without technical tools.

Too much dataEarth observation archives contain years of imagery and measurements that are hard to compare consistently.
Weak conclusionsA visible difference is not automatically a statistically meaningful trend.
High barrier to entryStudents and communities need a simple way to ask questions about places they know.
Kasa turns a location into a transparent, explorable environmental question.

03 / The science

Statistical honesty is part of the interface.

Kasa uses NASA POWER monthly temperature and precipitation data for the selected location.

  • Removes the seasonal cycle first
  • Aggregates monthly anomalies by year
  • Uses Mann-Kendall for monotonic trend
  • Uses Theil-Sen for a robust slope

The result is communicated without overclaiming.

  • Shows the fitted change and p-value
  • Explains the uncertainty of short windows
  • Never presents correlation as causation
  • Supports a 10-year and 40-year view
A trend is a statistical description of this window - not proof of what caused it.

05 / Why it matters

Make Earth observation understandable, not intimidating.

Kasa is designed for people who want to investigate environmental change but do not have a remote-sensing workstation or a statistics background.

  • Students can learn from places they recognize.
  • Communities can explore local evidence.
  • Researchers can inspect the sources and method.
  • Everyone sees the caveats alongside the result.

The intended impact

Help more people ask better questions about a changing planet - and make the limits of an answer as visible as the answer itself.

Look closer. Compare fairly. Claim only what the evidence supports.
